# Draft poster questions (to be agreed by the PIs)

**Status: draft.** As Marten noted, agreeing on the questions takes effort. These drafts are a starting point. Pick about **6 posters** for 30–40 participants, so that voting fits in a 20-minute coffee break.

The questions draw on the project's three pillars (cryptography, software security, physical/hardware security) and nine core challenges, as listed in the TU/e kick-off article of 30 Oct 2024 (see `CiCS-project/CiCS-output`, `project/overview.md`).

Two axis pairs are used:
- **A. Impact × Feasibility**: for "should CiCS do X?" questions.
- **B. Importance for CiCS × My own involvement**: for "where should our effort go?" questions. People find each other here: everyone in the top-right wants to work on the same thing.

Each axis runs from low (left/bottom) to high (right/top).

## Candidate questions

| # | Question | Axes | Notes |

| 1 | Should CiCS develop a joint proof-of-concept around **privacy-preserving medical data analysis**? | A | Marten's original example; connects MPC, ML and hardware |

## Spare formats

- **Blank posters** (2): for questions raised on the day; a participant writes the question and the axes.
- **Optional overview poster**: the 3×3 grid of the nine core challenges. Everyone places one note on the challenge closest to their own work. It works as an icebreaker and as a map of who does what.
